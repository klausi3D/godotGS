    ADR: What painterly is and where it lives (decision D-1)¶

    • Status: Accepted — decided by the owner on 2026-11-10. The owner's approving review and merge of the PR that adds this ADR confirm that the recorded text matches the decision.
    • Date: 2026-11-10.
    • Risk: this document is R0 (docs/**). It changes no code. The work it schedules is R3: per-splat stylisation edits binning and raster shaders, and moving the setting off GaussianSplatNode3D changes public API.
    • Refs: decision D-1 of the 2026-11 codebase audit; findings I-01, I-02, I-04, I-05, I-06, I-07 and F-11; issues #323 (inert painterly material data) and #1001 (blend strength).
    • Related ADRs: One route, many demand producers, whose decision 1 forbids features that work only with one node.
    • Paths are relative to modules/gaussian_splatting/. Each claim below was re-read at b58bbe35d1f.

    1. Problem¶

    GodotGS is for making games, and its first game needs a painterly look across a streamed world and the objects in it. Today the look has three problems.

    1. It is set per node and dies in a shared scene. GaussianSplatNode3D binds a painterly/* property group (painterly/enabled, painterly/edge_threshold, painterly/stroke_opacity, painterly/stroke_width, painterly/temporal_blend, painterly/seed) in nodes/gaussian_splat_node_3d.cpp. The renderer is shared by every splat node in a World3D, and apply_renderer_settings() in nodes/gaussian_splat_node_helpers.cpp turns painterly off whenever _is_renderer_shared_with_other_content() is true: more than one instance, or any world submission. A world plus one object therefore never shows the look, and nothing warns (I-01).
    2. It is a screen-space filter, not stylised splats. The live effect is a 3×3 luma Sobel pass (shaders/sobel_outline.glsl) followed by a blur along the edge tangent (shaders/brush_accumulate.glsl), both registered in shaders/compile_shaders.py. The PainterlyMaterial palette textures, noise LUTs and stroke density are uploaded and cached in interfaces/painterly_renderer.cpp (cached_palette_textures, cached_noise_luts, cached_stroke_density_buffer) but no pass binds them. The per-splat payload fields (palette ids, brush axes, painterly flags, stroke ages) are stored and saved, and a comment in shaders/tile_binning.glsl states that nothing in any shader reads them. shaders/includes/painterly_features.glsl is included only by shaders/gaussian_splat.frag.glsl, which no pipeline compiles; a test reads it as text (I-04).
    3. Its knobs do not do what they say. painterly/temporal_blend is passed to the renderer as a display gamma (I-05). The composite blend strength is the constant 1.0 in PainterlyRenderer (I-06). PainterlyRenderer::populate_painterly_gbuffer() is a second hand-written TileRenderParams producer that diverges from the main one (I-07). The HLOD streaming route refuses painterly outright: GaussianSplatRenderer::hlod_streaming_unsupported_reason() returns unsupported_view_or_material when painterly is on (I-02).

    F-11 (renderer-wide settings exposed as per-node properties that only the settings-owner node applies) waits on the same question: where painterly settings live.

    2. Options¶

    The options are those of decision D-1 in the audit.

    Option A: keep the screen-space look as a camera or environment effect¶

    The Sobel and brush passes stay. The setting moves off the node to WorldEnvironment or a dedicated environment resource.

    • Cost: small. Remove the per-node ownership and the force-off.
    • Enables: a painterly look on the world and all objects within weeks.
    • Limits: it is an edge filter. It does not stylise splats, and the palette, noise and brush material features stay unimplemented.

    Option B: per-splat stylisation¶

    Brush shape, orientation, palette and noise are applied in binning and raster, driven by PainterlyMaterial.

    • Cost: an R3 rendering project: a design ADR plus months of work.
    • Enables: a look that no shipped engine offers. The audit's survey of the state of the art found no shipped painterly splat renderer (marked unconfirmed there).
    • Limits and risk: no peer exists to copy, so the target look needs reference images that the owner signs. Nothing new reaches the screen until the first slice lands.

    Option C: both¶

    Do A now, and B as a v1.0 track with its own ADR.

    • Cost: A's cost plus B's, and A's work is thrown away when B replaces it.
    • Enables: the game and a world-plus-object scene get a painterly look now; B follows.

    Audit recommendation: C. In every option the setting leaves GaussianSplatNode3D.

    Owner decision¶

    • Option chosen: B, per-splat stylisation only. There is no interim screen-space effect at camera or environment level.
    • Decided by: the owner, 2026-11-10.
    • Recorded in: the owner's answer in the 2026-11-10 session, recorded in this ADR. The owner's approving review and merge of the pull request that adds this ADR confirm that the recorded text matches the decision.
    • Rejected:
    • A, because it keeps an edge filter as the product look and leaves the material features unimplemented. Its advantage, a look on world plus objects within weeks, is given up.
    • C (the recommendation), because its interim half is work on a pass that B replaces. The trade-off accepted: no painterly look in a world-plus-object scene until per-splat stylisation lands.

    3. Decision¶

    1. Painterly is per-splat stylisation driven by PainterlyMaterial: palette, noise, brush axis and stroke density are bound in binning and raster.
    2. Ownership moves off GaussianSplatNode3D. Per-splat stylisation must work on the world and on objects in the same frame, so a per-node setting that the shared renderer overrides cannot stay. The ownership part of the PT02 epic stays in the plan.
    3. The existing Sobel and brush pass stays as it is until per-splat stylisation replaces it. It gets no further investment: no new knobs, no fixes to its existing knobs, no migration to an environment-level effect.
    4. Per-splat stylisation needs its own R3 design ADR before any shader change (CLN-PT05-1). This record does not specify the shader design.
    5. Reference images are a prerequisite. The owner signs a set of reference images that define the target look before any implementation slice of per-splat stylisation starts. CLN-PT05-1 proposes the scene set and the command that produces the images; the owner signs both.

    Migration of existing scenes¶

    Under every option the painterly/* properties leave GaussianSplatNode3D. Scenes that set painterly/enabled then need one of two treatments:

    • keep painterly/enabled as a deprecated alias that maps to the new owner and warns once; or
    • remove it, with a release note.

    API stability allows removal without a deprecation period before v1.0. The owner has not chosen between the two. The task that moves ownership proposes one in its PR, and the owner decides there.

    4. Consequences for the cleanup plan¶

    Task Effect
    CLN-PT05 (epic) and CLN-PT05-1, -2, -3 Confirmed. Per-splat stylisation is the painterly track. PT05-1 (design ADR with signed reference images) comes first.
    CLN-PT02 (epic) Ownership part stays: the setting leaves GaussianSplatNode3D.
    CLN-PT02-1 Stays as the ownership task. Its contract assumes an environment-level screen-space owner (A or C); it must be re-scoped to option B before dispatch.
    CLN-PT02-2 (gamma and temporal_blend naming) Void: polish of the screen-space pass.
    CLN-PT02-3 (blend strength, #1001) Void: polish of the screen-space pass.
    Findings I-05, I-06 (and F-10) Move from PT02 to PT05: the knobs disappear when PT05 replaces the pass.

    Findings and their route after this decision:

    • I-01 (force-off in a shared scene): CLN-PT02, ownership move.
    • I-02 (HLOD route refuses painterly): the HLOD refusal list, see One route, many demand producers.
    • I-04 (inert material data): CLN-PT05.
    • I-05, I-06: CLN-PT05.
    • I-07 (second TileRenderParams producer): CLN-RT02.
    • F-11 (renderer-wide settings on nodes): CLN-RS01. Painterly settings no longer sit on the node, so F-11 no longer has to account for them.

    5. Out of scope¶

    • Any code, shader, setting or test change. Each follow-up task cites this ADR.
    • The per-splat shader design, data layout and memory rule (CLN-PT05-1).
    • The new owner of the painterly settings: material, project setting or another resource (the ownership task, under this decision).
    • The compatibility treatment of painterly/enabled (see Migration above).
    • Whether the HLOD route lifts its painterly refusal for the existing screen-space pass, or only for per-splat stylisation. This decision forbids new investment in the pass; the owner has not ruled on the HLOD-route task that touches it.
